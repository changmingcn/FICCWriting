# FICCOutline rationale and distinctions

This file holds the rationale, examples, and history moved out of the FICCOutline.md body text, in sections by the body's section numbers. Each entry first gives the original sentence in a block quote, then one sentence on which rule in the body it supports. Here each original sentence appears in English translation, and Chinese phrases inside it stay in Chinese with an English gloss. The Chinese originals are in references/zh/FICCOutline-rationale.md. Consult it as needed while writing.

## Introduction

> Since every node states its task, the outline does more than guide the writing: when new stories, counter-views, and new material come in, it also shows which branch they belong on and which branch to change;

Supports O62, O46: because every node states its task, incoming material shows which branch it goes on and which branch to change. Provenance: user 10-12 (posted text: judging where a new story goes).

> The lead and the headings are what its root and its second layer look like on the page.

Supports O76: the root becomes the lead, and the second layer becomes the headings.

## 1 The outline is a tree

> Branches are sorted along the trunk; tributaries are ordered from the source.

Supports O01: a figurative way of saying that each block is sorted along the layer above, in a tree structure.

### 1.1 Nodes and scales

> These names are not a hierarchy table, still less a cap on the number of layers:

Supports O02, O24: the eight scales are only names, and the content sets the number of layers. Provenance: user 10-10 (original words: “不是‘全篇—章节—段落—句子’四层结构” (not a four-layer structure of ‘whole piece–chapter–paragraph–sentence’)).

> A block of content may be only a branch line at the level above, and it is the trunk of this place when unfolded.

Supports O03: the same block of content is a branch line at the level above and a trunk when unfolded. Provenance: user 10-09 （二）.

> So the same block of text is both a child of its parent node and the parent of its own child nodes,

Supports O03: the same block of text holds both the parent role and the child role, so the rules treat it the same in both.

### 1.3 Every subtree is complete

> This is the completeness invariant among the seven invariants.

Supports O05, O41: subtree completeness is the completeness invariant among the seven invariants.

> Once the shape of the tree is set, first establish its root.

Supports O06: the original's transition sentence from 1 to 2.

## 2 The root claim: the center of the outline

> Most FICC texts are argumentative,

Supports O07: most FICC texts are argumentative, so the root claim is mostly the whole piece's judgment.

### 2.1 Define the question, then form the judgment

> Those one or two sentences are the whole piece's judgment.

Supports O11: when the root claim is as short as one or two sentences, those sentences are the whole piece's judgment.

### 2.2 Work the mechanism through

> A real mechanism may have parallel channels, feedback, common causes, and different time scales.

Supports O14: real mechanisms have several channels and feedback, so the narrative picks one route and does not write it as the only causal chain.

> Between the two ends of any mechanism there are intermediate conditions,

Supports O15: there are intermediate conditions between a mechanism's two ends, so a missing step must be filled in.

> For example, from policy to lending.

Example, supports O15: there are intermediate conditions between policy and lending.

## 3 Every node states four things clearly

> Once there is a center, every other node on the outline must state itself as clearly as the root does, and must also state its relations upward and sideways; so the outline is not a list of headings:

Supports O18: only when every node states itself as the root does, and states its relations upward and sideways, is the outline more than a list of headings. Provenance: user 10-12 (posted text: an outline cannot be only a list of headings).

### 3.1 The four things

> The first two belong to the node itself; the last two belong to the node and the tree;

Supports O18: the first two belong to the node itself, and the last two to the node and the tree.

> An outline that writes only the first two is a string of conclusions; it does not show how they combine into one argument,

Supports O18: with only the first two, you cannot see how the conclusions combine into one argument, so all four must be answerable.

### 3.2 The claim has content and covers everything

> This rule is most easily missed at the upper layers: at the paragraph level, conclusions already get written, but at parts and sections the text slips back to “这一部分讨论……” (this part discusses…) and “这一节说明……” (this section explains…).

Supports O20, O86: the upper layers most easily miss the conclusion, so check the openings of parts and sections first. Provenance: user 10-11 （六） (gist of the original words: the paragraphs are fine now, but the overall architecture and the main sub-points are not yet).

> When the body text says “似乎” (seemingly), “据报道” (reportedly), or “部分与会者认为可能” (some participants think it possible), the claim cannot be written as “已经” (already) or “显然” (clearly).

Example, supports O22: the body text's qualifiers decide how strongly the claim may be stated.

> If the opening cannot give a conclusion with content, the child nodes below, however neatly arranged, will not add up to one argument.

Supports O20: without a conclusion with content in the opening, no neat arrangement of child nodes adds up to an argument.

### 3.4 Unfold only as far as needed

> One branch line needs only a sentence; another needs a group of paragraphs or several subsections,

Example, supports O24: branch lines unfold to different depths according to their content.

> Once the way to write a node is set, the outline can grow from the root downward, layer by layer.

Supports O25: the original's transition sentence from 3 to 4.

## 4 Build the outline top-down

### 4.2 How to write the outline, and where it goes

> The argument is the body text's business.

Supports O31: leave the argument to the body text, and do not write it on the outline.

> With this table, two writers working in parallel still write the same set of names.

Supports O32: the name table makes parallel writers use the same set of names.

(O33) The check reports conditional and unconditional entries separately.

(O38) ficc-hand's scripts/tieout.py reads the `- **主旨**：` and `## 名字表` forms and reports other forms as 未检查 (not checked).

> Once the outline is checked, the body text only unfolds each node into a complete small article.

Supports O89: once the outline is checked, the body text only unfolds each node into a complete small article.

### 4.3 Summarizing and length

> Of the seven invariants, three (summarizing, completeness, and length) govern whether the tree itself is right, and belong to this file (for completeness, see 1.3);

Supports O41: of the seven invariants, summarizing, completeness, and length belong to this file.

> The other four (grouping, attachment, hanging, and re-mention) govern whether content is placed right: for attachment and hanging, see 5.3; for grouping and re-mention, see FICCStructure.md 3.2 and FICCVoice.md V44.

Supports O41: the other four govern whether content is placed right. For attachment and hanging, see 5.3. For grouping and re-mention, see FICCStructure.md 3.2 and FICCVoice.md V44.

(O41) When any layer breaks one of them, rewording cannot repair it.

> A built outline is not final: when new stories and material come in, they must first find a place on the tree;

Supports O62, O46: a built outline is not final, and new material must first find a place on the tree. Provenance: user 10-12 (posted text: the tree may be adjusted).

> When counter-views and new material change the judgment, the outline must change too.

Supports O62: when counter-views and new material change the judgment, the outline must change too. Provenance: user 10-12 (posted text).

## 5 Placement: where new stories and changes go on the tree

### 5.3 Attachment and hanging

> A case's measurement-basis explanation serves the case first;

Example, supports O52: the measurement-basis explanation serves the case first.

> The case serves a local mechanism;

Example, supports O52: the case serves a local mechanism.

> The local mechanism in turn supports the whole piece's judgment.

Example, supports O52: the local mechanism in turn supports the whole piece's judgment.

> When a contrast (for example, that cell for another market) neither supports nor rebuts the main line, hang it after the main line is proven and before the verdict, or put it in an appendix; do not make it a part at the same level as the trunk sub-points.

Example moved from O53: the parenthetical “that cell for another market” is an example of a contrast.

> Among the material, contrary evidence is what most likely changes the tree, and the root claim is already shown in the lead,

Supports O54: contrary evidence most likely changes the tree, and the root claim is shown in the lead, so counter-evidence needs a home early.

> Telling the whole main line and then discussing counter-views together is one way of hanging, not a fixed order,

Supports O54, O53: finishing the main line and then discussing counter-views together is only one way of hanging, not a fixed order.

### 5.5 Facts, interpretation, and conclusions connect

> If new material changes the judgment, what must change is not just one position but the tree itself.

Supports O62: when new material changes the judgment, the tree itself must change. Provenance: user 10-12 (posted text: revise the structure when new material changes the central judgment).

## 6 Maintaining the outline: how counter-views and new material change the tree

> After the outline is built, two things are most likely to change it: first, evidence against the main line; second, newly arrived stories and material:

Supports O59: the two things most likely to change the outline are contrary evidence and new material. Provenance: user 10-12 (posted text).

### 6.1 Counter-views: give the strongest reason first, then weigh

> Short-term safety and long-term fragility can hold at the same time, and different markets can go opposite ways,

Supports O61: short-term safety and long-term fragility can coexist, and different markets can go opposite ways, so explain why the differences change the outcome. Provenance: user 10-08 (basis: the main line is bearish, while a branch line holds that no blow-up will come in the short term).

### 6.3 Cross-branch annotations

> The maintenance rules are the same in every genre; only the outline's depth changes.

Supports O65: the original's transition sentence from 6 to 7.

## 7 Genre scaling and rewrite permissions

> Since changing genre changes only length,

Supports O65: changing genre changes only length, so the rules hold in every genre.

### 7.1 How each genre scales

> These files are not article templates; the four positions are a way of writing,

Supports O67: this skill's files are not article templates, and the four positions are a way of writing.

> Participant structure, size estimate, and mechanism process are one common unfolding for a financial case,

Supports O66: participant structure, size estimate, and mechanism process are a common unfolding for financial cases.

### 7.2 Expanding and compressing

> Smooth sentences after a deletion do not mean the argument is still complete.

Supports O68: smoothness after deletion is not completeness of argument, so compression must keep grounds and qualifiers.

### 7.3 Rewrite permissions

> When the boundaries hold, the revised draft is still the original author's judgment, only with clearer structure and easier wording.

Supports O69 to O75: when the boundaries hold, the revised draft is still the original author's judgment.

> Once genre has set the depth, the outline's top two layers must become what is read first on the page: the lead and the headings.

Supports O76: the original's transition sentence from 7 to 8.

## 10 Conclusion

> The order of these nine sections is the order in which one central sentence grows into an outline tree, gives new material its place, is maintained, and then lands on the page.

Supports the whole file: the order of the nine sections is the order in which the outline grows from the center into a tree, places material, is maintained, and lands on the page.

> The outline's use is to give every piece of material a clear position, every position a clear task, and every layer of unfolding relations the reader can understand;

Supports O46, O51: the outline gives every piece of material a position, every position a task, and every layer of unfolding a relation. Provenance: user 10-12 (posted text, near-verbatim: 每一份材料都有明确的位置，每一个位置都有明确的任务，每一层展开都有可以理解的关系 (every piece of material has a clear position, every position has a clear task, and every layer of unfolding has understandable relations)).

> In this way, a story's weight comes both from itself and from the explanatory, persuasive, or narrative force it produces together with other content.

Supports O46, O48: a story's weight also comes from the force it produces together with other content. Provenance: user 10-12 (posted text, near-verbatim: 一个故事的价值既来自它本身，也来自它与其他内容共同产生的解释力、说服力或叙事力量 (a story's value comes both from itself and from the explanatory, persuasive, or narrative force it produces together with other content)).

> So the outline is what is delivered before writing begins:

Supports O89: the outline is delivered before writing begins.

> What cannot be seen on the outline will not grow by itself in the body text.

Supports O89: what the outline does not show, the body text will not grow.
