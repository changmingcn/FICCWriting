# FICCCompose: Organize paragraphs and the piece

FICCCompose governs, inside a settled outline node, how the text opens, carries over, unfolds, moves between layers, pays off what it previews, and closes. FICCOutline.md decides placement, selection, claims, order, headings, and length. FICCStructure.md decides relation types and whether a relation holds. FICCVoice.md decides sentences: wording, connectives, terms, punctuation, and voice. Where a rule below names a connective or a marker, it shows where the relation is marked. FICCVoice.md chooses the words.

- Inputs: the outline node in force (claim, task, order, relation and unfolding lines, length cap, full-statement places), the relation sheet, the material excerpts the packet attaches, the authorized scope, and the adjacent body text.
- Output: the paragraph draft. Add no separate paragraph plan.
- Within the authorized scope, split and merge paragraphs, and adjust unfolding within and between paragraphs. Do not change outline nodes, settled relations, or a fixed order. Do not build a paragraph outline that competes with the outline for claims, grouping, or order.
- New material that has not yet got a place on the effective outline is not written into the body; report it per FICCVoice.md V04 for the outline agent. A first draft from an existing outline needs no separate placement record (FICCStructure.md S75).
- Under a grant limited to splitting or merging paragraphs, add no sentences and keep sentence order. A paragraph left without its claim at the opening is reported as a to-do, not written.
- Hand back gaps and conflicts per FICCVoice.md V04. Rewrite permissions: FICCOutline.md 7.3. The fidelity duties of FICCOutline.md O70 and FICCVoice.md V38 hold here too.
- “The user” means the author. “You” means the agent applying the rule.

## 1 Paragraph openings

- **C01** **At every layer, the conclusion comes at the opening.**
  - Give the reader first the whole needed to place the details.
  - A paragraph opening must be the paragraph's claim or its topic. The claim is the judgment the paragraph argues. The topic is what the paragraph is about.
  - The first sentence not only governs what follows but also states the view itself.
  - The sentences after it explain, reason out, illustrate, add evidence to, or qualify it. Write them per C02, FICCVoice.md V22, and C09.
  - Where the opening is:
    - A paragraph's opening is its first sentence, together with the second sentence when the first only sets the tone or, in an argument paragraph, states the claim without its main reasons. The second sentence then carries the main reasons (C02, table row 1).
    - The opening of a paragraph group, section, or part is its first paragraph.
    - When a heading states a judgment, the first paragraph below it must still give the conclusion first.
    - When the heading is a topic or a posed question, the section's first sentence must give the judgment.
    - The whole piece's opening is the title plus the lead. For the latest paragraph where the answer may land, see C03.
    - A subsection of only one to three paragraphs gets no separate claim paragraph. Then the first sentence of the first paragraph (with the carry-over sentence when it only sets the tone) carries the claim.
    - When a part's opening paragraph sits right next to the first section's opening paragraph, give only the part's claim and the chain linking the sections.
    - A sub-point inside a paragraph opens with its own first sentence. An evidence block that only supports the judgment right before it writes no claim sentence of its own. It attaches in place and counts as 顺承 for FICCVoice.md V22.
  - Replace material lists with content judgments.
  - Do not cram all details into the first sentence.
  - Do not write previews that only announce the subject, such as “下面讲X” (below we discuss X) or “这一块讲X” (this block discusses X).
  - Do not put a material sentence first:
    - A list of what one firm said, or of who said what.
    - A number or fact that is only material, not the judgment this paragraph argues.
  - When all the following sentences discuss that material, also keep it out of the paragraph opening.
  - Move a conclusion reached only at the paragraph's end (“所以……” (so…), “可见……” (evidently…)) to the opening as the claim. Do not repeat it at the end.
  - After moving a conclusion forward, rewrite its seams with reasons, evidence, and qualifiers one by one. Do not just move the conclusion and pile the old material after a colon.
  - Open by the two tables below.

    Paragraph type (decides what the opening states and whether a main reason is required):

    | Row | Type of paragraph or block | What the opening states |
    |---|---|---|
    | 1 | Argument paragraph (default) | The claim plus its main reasons. Reading only the opening, the reader knows what it asserts and on what main grounds. |
    | 2 | Explanatory paragraph, or a topic stating the current state | The topic: what state the subject is in right now, such as “……有很大争议” (… is highly contested) or “……分两头” (… splits two ways). Main reasons are not required. |
    | 5 | First-hand case box | The story or facts first. |
    | 6 | Story, scene, or post-mortem of one's own errors | May follow the course of events and put the judgment at the end. The opening still tells the reader what the story shows and which matter it went wrong on. Do not open empty. |
    | 7 | Disagreement paragraph (the disagreement itself is the point) | The first sentence states the disagreement as the topic. The parties follow in turn, each marking its relation to the previous one (turn and so on). |

    Opening form (decides only in which sentence the claim lands):

    | Row | Form of the opening | Where the claim lands |
    |---|---|---|
    | 3 | Hook opening (lead or part opening) | Hook first, with the claim right after the hook. A hook is a price, a trade, a story, or a scene. A popular claim, a reader's question, or a real first-hand experience also counts as a hook. |
    | 4 | Posed question, link to earlier text, or concession first | The second sentence completes the claim (sub-items below, C13). |
    | 8 | Long piece | It may give its judgment in two sentences: the short-term conclusion first, the long-term conclusion at the end of the same paragraph. |

    - An argument paragraph that opens with a hook, a posed question or a concession still carries its main reason, in the sentence that completes the claim.
    - Table row 4:
      - With a concession first, the first sentence says where the key may not lie, and only the second says where it really lies.
      - With a link to earlier text, the first sentence corrects or ties back to the previous block's ending, and the second gives this block's claim.
      - With a posed question, you may first tie back to the frame and then ask. The answer follows right after the question.
    - For how to write a hook, see FICCVoice.md 6.
    - Table row 7: the rule in FICCVoice.md 5.1 that keeps speakers within the sentence governs only who is the subject, not what goes at the paragraph opening.
  - Place headings, the lead, carry-overs, and the close where the outline sets them.
  - See references/FICCVoice-rationale.md Introduction and C01. See references/FICCStructure-rationale.md 6.1, 7.1.
- **C02** **Test the paragraph opening against what follows, item by item.**
  - When both sides of a contrast or turn belong to this paragraph, make the opening hold both sides.
  - When the first sentence does not yet complete this paragraph's judgment, the next sentence completes it. Whether that sentence must carry the main reason follows the C01 paragraph-type table.
  - When the opening already states the view in full, add no carry-over. Continue with one sentence. When the previous block's close already lays out this block's question, also continue with one sentence.
  - When the lead's claim is already complete and the carry-over paragraph is dropped, make the chain between parts readable in the lead.
  - Every sub-point that the opening or the carry-over sentence previews is paid off by the text below (C20).
  - Check: attach each judgment after the opening. Point out which part of the opening it explains, supports, limits, or rebuts.
    - If it serves only another judgment, do not keep it in this paragraph as material.
    - If it belongs to the settled claim but the opening does not state it fully, complete the opening.
    - If the claim or attachment must change, hand it back per FICCVoice.md V04.
  - See references/FICCVoice-rationale.md C02.
- **C03** **Show the whole article's claim at the start of the lead. Do not make the reader read half the piece to learn what we argue.**
  - The lead has three arrangements:
    - Situation and conflict first, then the answer.
    - The answer first, then situation and conflict.
    - The reader's question first: first say what the previous issue said and what readers ask.
  - In the first two, the answer falls within the first one or two paragraphs.
  - The third then gives the new situation and the pessimists' view, and closes with a posed question.
  - In the third, the answer is the next paragraph's first sentence, and no later than the first sentence of the lead's third paragraph.
  - When the lead mentions the pessimists' view, leave the evidence list to the counter-view section (see FICCOutline.md O53).
  - See C04: the carry-over paragraph after the lead.
  - See FICCVoice.md 6: the hook in the first paragraph.

## 2 Carry-overs and chains

- **C04** **A carry-over is not a table of contents. Arrange a carry-over paragraph as a chain that goes deeper.**
  - Carry-over paragraphs mean the carry-over paragraph after the lead, carry-over paragraphs at the openings of parts and sections, and carry-over sentences within paragraphs.
  - Each step is derived from the previous step, or goes one layer further.
  - Mark with connectives which step it adds.
  - Each trunk block takes one step on the chain, in the chain's order.
  - Only when blocks cannot form a progression (a line from shallow to deep, from appearance to cause, or from general to specific), lay them out flat (mark the items per C13).
  - When different aspects are laid out flat, open with topic frames. The aspects use one basis of division, such as volume and price, assets and liabilities, or issuers and investors.
  - In a style-only edit or a faithful rewrite, if the original cannot form a progressing chain, lay it out flat.
  - Check: a carry-over after which no overall view can be stated is a table of contents. Report it for rearranging per FICCVoice.md V04.
    - A table of contents looks like blocks laid out flat, each stating one conclusion, joined only by “另外” (in addition) and “还有” (also).
- **C05** **A carry-over paragraph arranges only the trunk.**
  - “反方看法” (counter-views), the conclusion section, and appendices that hang after the main line do not enter the carry-over paragraph.
  - When they reach their own positions, link them with linking-back sentences (hanging rules: see FICCOutline.md 5.3; the whole piece's close: per C18).
  - Each step of the carry-over has its own conclusion.
  - A main-line sentence already said in the previous paragraph serves only as support for this step.
- **C06** **A carry-over sits one layer above the child nodes. It states only each step's conclusion and the relations between steps.**
  - A carry-over does not carry the child nodes' own numbers, quotations, or examples.
  - A carry-over before several cases first states what they prove together.
  - A carry-over states conclusions. It does not only report routes, coordinates, or counts.
    - Routes, such as what comes first and what comes next.
    - Coordinates, such as see part such-and-such.
    - Counts, such as there are these several features.
  - Write an upper-layer carry-over as one paragraph, not as several paragraphs of roadmap.
  - How the steps previewed in a carry-over are paid off: per C20.
  - Check: cover the child nodes and read only the carry-over. You can state this block's overall view in one sentence.
  - Then check each step by the group's arrangement (chain, progression or parallel) as the relation sheet sets it, not the seam-level relation. In a dependency chain, check that each step's premise is given before it. In a progression group, check the layer each step adds. In a parallel group, check the shared question and each item's own contribution.
  - Report a step that fails its check per FICCVoice.md V04.
  - See references/FICCStructure-rationale.md 6.2.
- **C07** **A re-mention must carry one more layer. This rule governs only section level and above.**
  - The parent node's carry-over gives each trunk child node one step on the chain.
  - A child node's opening may re-mention this step in more concrete terms.
  - A re-mention must carry one more layer (the conclusions or reasons of the blocks beneath it).
  - The same conclusion appears no more than twice in the openings of adjacent layers.
    - That is, once in the upper layer's carry-over and once in this layer's claim.
  - Gathering it back in a close with something new does not count as re-mention.

## 3 Paragraph tasks and paragraph groups

- **C08** **Choose the unfolding method by the settled relation.**
  - Parallel group: write each item's conclusion first. Then attach its grounds and qualifiers.
    - Each sub-point is a block of its own.
    - After giving numbers, state what is worth seeing here.
  - Transmission chain: make each step's result the object or condition of the next step.
  - Explanations, examples, and qualifiers: place them right after the judgment they serve. Do not promote them to parallel points.
  - When an item itself contains these layers, keep separating them inside the item.
    - A paragraph may hold several groups of sentences, such as one on conditions and another giving examples. Organize each group of sentences around its own trunk.
    - Within an example, further separate premise and result.
    - Do not read “一段一棵树” (one paragraph, one tree) as every sentence hanging directly on the paragraph opening.
  - While still adding reasons, examples, or qualifiers for the current judgment, you may stay in the same paragraph.
  - When different subjects together complete one comparison, they may share a paragraph.
  - When moving to a sub-question that needs its own unfolding, carry it over in a new paragraph within the settled relation.
  - Do not split each explanation into an isolated short paragraph.
  - Paragraphs: make sure each paragraph has only one topic. Keep each paragraph to no more than ten sentences.
  - Reorganize paragraphs by relation, not by length.
    - A paragraph may hold several facts and actions, provided the reader knows how they jointly advance the argument at this point.
    - Where a paragraph has turned to another question that can unfold on its own, or switches back and forth between several comparison bases, reorganize by relation.
    - With reorganization permission, split it into paragraphs that carry over from one another.
    - When a paragraph exceeds ten sentences, carry over into a new paragraph at an argument-unit boundary.
  - A new draft may split sentences and paragraphs on this basis. A revision stays within the permissions in FICCVoice.md V04.
  - See references/FICCVoice-rationale.md C08.
- **C09** **Each paragraph does one main thing for the layer above: put forward a judgment, explain a concept, show a scene, analyze evidence, handle an objection, form a conclusion, or drive action.**
  - A paragraph may hold several facts and several actions, but only one main action.
  - The first sentence lets the reader know which of these the paragraph is doing and what it concludes (C01).
  - Between two adjacent paragraphs, have an advance you can name.
    - What step the later paragraph adds beyond the earlier one.
  - Five common advances: judgment→explanation→grounds, story→analysis→insight, phenomenon→mechanism→impact, assertion→counterexample→revision, scene→character reaction→new action.
  - These five are common routes, not templates. The actual relation between two paragraphs is per the relation sheet.
  - Check: if you cannot name a paragraph's main action, treat it as several pieces of material stuck together.
  - Check: if you cannot name what two paragraphs advance, treat them as doing the same thing.
  - Fix both yourself within the authorized scope: split, merge, or reorder the paragraphs inside the node (C08).
    - Only when the fix would change which node the material belongs to, a claim, or a fixed order, report it per FICCVoice.md V04 for T0 to pass to the outline agent (FICCOutline.md 5).
    - Send a doubtful relation to the relation agent per the hand-back table in SKILL.md 3.
- **C10** **Build a group of paragraphs on the same four positions.**
  - The first paragraph is the group's claim paragraph. Its first sentence states the group's conclusion.
  - The carry-over sentence arranges the following paragraphs as a chain, stating each paragraph's conclusion and their relations.
  - The middle paragraphs are sub-points.
  - The last paragraph or sentence closes (optional, per C17), stating what the group resolved and why the next group follows.
  - When more internal unfolding is needed, you may carry it with ranked paragraph groups, leading sentences, and local bullet points.
  - “第一，” (first,) may stand inside a sentence (two items) or open a paragraph. When it opens a paragraph, the sentence after it is this item's claim and must carry information.
  - Use a colon subheading (“第一个办法：”, the first method:) only when each item runs to several paragraphs and the reader needs headings to find them again.
- **C11** **In the several paragraph groups of one subsection, each group continues to unfold around one local question.**
  - What the next paragraph picks up from the previous one is readable from content and cohesion.
    - It picks up the conclusion, a question left open, or a limit on it.
  - When moving to another subject, point in time, measurement basis, or scale of judgment, state the change clearly.
  - Where paragraphs only take turns reporting institution names, financing numbers, and new cases, first decide what they show together.
    - Then decide the order.
  - Adding “此外” (besides) or “与此同时” (meanwhile) to each paragraph is not reorganization.
  - State a shared definition or background only once, and use it concretely afterward. Where to put definitions: see FICCVoice.md 5.1.
- **C12** **“压缩” (compress) cuts only wording and detail.**
  - A story's length, level of detail, narrative angle, and order of appearance are all decided by its task in the whole tree.
  - Where the cap cannot hold the facts the placement record keeps, return a placement objection (FICCOutline.md O58).
  - See references/FICCStructure-rationale.md 9.1.

## 4 Transitions between layers, signposts, and closes

- **C13** **At each seam, state how the preceding item connects to the new judgment.**
  - From the relation sheet, name which conclusion of the previous block you carry over from.
  - The first sentence of a child node ties back to the previous block's conclusion, with a relation word or back-reference. The second sentence (or the same sentence) gives this block's claim.
  - When turning to the counter-view, the first sentence ties back and turns; the second gives the first item's conclusion at once.
  - Between parts, first summarize the previous part, then advance. Between genre stages, also mark the relation in the linking-back sentence.
  - Parallel child nodes of the same kind also need linking: from the second item on, mark the add-on relation to the preceding item on top of any numbering. Which marker: FICCVoice.md V08, V22.
  - What a linking-back sentence connects to in the previous block: per FICCVoice.md V53, as with paragraph-opening connectives.
  - Write per the settled relation:
    - Cause and effect: through what action, constraint, or channel the first item changes the second.
    - Inference: what is known, and so how far one can judge.
    - Turn or concession: what judgment the first item leads one to form, and which part of it the second item changes or limits.
    - Progression: which further layer of explanation it adds beyond the first item.
    - Parallel or comparison: what question the items answer together, and on what shared dimension they are compared.
  - Write the connection into actual statements. Do not substitute “形成支撑、构成闭环” (forms support, closes the loop).
  - Check: after restating both items, you can say why they connect this way. If you cannot, hand it back per FICCVoice.md V04.
- **C14** **Allocate markers between layers by scale. Above section level, let four devices together make the relations readable.**
  - At paragraph level and below, mark relations fully with connectives: which seam and how densely: FICCVoice.md V22.
  - At section level and above, first lay out by genre stage: lead, trunk, counter-views, conclusion section.
  - Mark relations above section level with these four devices:
    - Judgment headings: reading only the heading tells what the block asserts (how to write them: see FICCOutline.md 8.2).
    - Position: contrast, counter-views, and the conclusion section each in its place.
    - Carry-over paragraphs: arrange the blocks as one chain and mark what each step adds.
    - Linking-back sentences: pay off the carry-over paragraph at the opening of each block.
  - Put relation words in linking-back sentences, not in headings. Do not hang empty labels.
  - See references/FICCStructure-rationale.md 6.3.
- **C15** **A signpost sentence points only to the block right after it. A stray block is reported per FICCVoice.md V04 for re-hanging; do not move it yourself.**
  - This rule governs only signpost sentences. How cross-branch foreshadowing lands on the page: decide it here, from the endpoints the relation sheet gives. Carry it with named content. Do not write signpost sentences such as “留到……讲” (left for… to discuss).
  - Report per FICCVoice.md V04 a block that sits inside the main line but is not a link of it. It moves per the hanging rules (FICCOutline.md 5.3).
  - Do not reconnect the line by adding transition sentences.
  - See references/FICCStructure-rationale.md 6.3.
- **C16** **Before a branch line unfolds, introduce it; while it unfolds, stay on topic; when it ends, return the result.**
  - Before it unfolds, let the reader know why the text turns here for now.
  - When it ends, return the result to the layer that directly governs it.
  - A short branch line returns through the meaning of adjacent sentences, without adding “这说明” (this shows) every time.
  - For a longer branch line, or one that turns several times, state explicitly which question the text has returned to.
  - A group of opposing forces may be wrapped up together. Do not repeat the whole piece's conclusion item by item.
- **C17** **Make every summary and close trace back to existing content.**
  - In the close, gather only the judgments already established in this block, with their limits.
  - When the close moves forward, advance only as far as the material supports.
  - The close gathers the child nodes back into this block's claim and moves one step forward. Moving one step forward means saying what it implies or leaves behind, or handing the reader to the next block.
  - Do not restate the claim in other words.
  - The whole piece and each part must have a close. A section with three or more child nodes (subsections, paragraph groups, or paragraphs) needs a close. A section made of several numbered stories needs an overall opening and a close, however many stories there are (FICCOutline.md 1.3). Closes below section level are optional.
  - A list-style monthly report with parallel sections also needs a close for the whole piece. The whole-piece close gathers the sections' conclusions in one paragraph, then moves one step forward.
  - Where carry-over is already clear, do not add mechanical transition sentences. Where the material carries an argument the text still lacks, add it; what the material lacks goes back per FICCVoice.md V04.
  - You may first say what the matter implies, then bring the stories back to this block's claim. A part's close may land on method.
  - The last child node's close may double as the parent node's close. When it doubles, gather the conclusions of all the parent node's child nodes, not only the last one.
  - A paragraph closes in its last one or two sentences.
  - A paragraph may end on a judgment, a summary, or an echo.
  - Do not stuff the middle with numbers of unknown purpose.
  - Check: against the sub-items, the opening leaves out no side. It does not state partial, possible, or short-term as total, certain, or long-term. Extract the openings and closes of each layer. They should read as one argument, with no unfounded strengthening.
  - See references/FICCStructure-rationale.md 6.4.
- **C18** **Land the whole piece's close on something grounded in earlier text. Do not recite the table of contents again.**
  - The whole piece may end on a judgment, a condition for holding, or the next item to watch.
  - The whole piece may end on a maxim or ghost story grounded in earlier text.
  - The closing sentence may name and gather several earlier subtrees, then give exit conditions.
  - When closing on a maxim or ghost story, put a summary before it, and place the key to understanding it in earlier text.
  - When closing on a last stand-alone story, the shared question and the earlier main line must already be laid out.
  - Do not let the ending become a place to park material with nowhere else to go.
- **C19** **Do not write every layer as “总起—分述—总之” (overall opening–itemized account–in sum).**
  - A narrative block may run by time internally.
  - Its parent node has a claim; the block itself closes with one sentence that states its meaning.
  - After a story, example, or scene, state in one sentence what it shows.
  - Delete tails hung at the end of a story that do not lead back to the story's conclusion.
  - Most paragraphs' close is simply the last sentence of the last sub-point.
  - Write “这意味着什么” (what this means) only when the block truly derives something earlier text lacks.
  - Do not force carry-overs and closes onto small nodes to fill a format.
  - Do not land the close on a life lesson with no inferential link to earlier text.
  - See references/FICCStructure-rationale.md 6.4.
  - See references/FICCStructure-rationale.md 2.4.

## 5 Preview and payoff, setups

- **C20** **As many steps as the claim and carry-over sentence preview, the sub-points pay off, in the same order.**
  - The connectives in the claim and carry-over sentence preview the relations among the sub-points below.
  - Each sub-point's first sentence pays it off with the matching marker.
  - After previewing “第一……第二……” (first… second…), open the items below with “第一，” and “第二，”.
  - When the carry-over sentence contains “而且” (moreover), the second sub-point is the one that goes a layer further.
  - With “虽然……但是……” (although… but…), write the conceded point first.
  - Write the point that turns back after it, opening with “但是” (but).
  - Check: after writing a paragraph, extract the connectives in the claim and carry-over sentence.
    - See whether the relations they preview match the actual unfolding of the sub-points.
  - See references/FICCStructure-rationale.md 8.3.
- **C21** **Pay off hook details later in the text.**

## 7 Compose checks

Run the Check lines of C01–C22 and C24 on the paragraph draft, then the whole-draft pass in C23. Sentence-level cold reading, AI tone, and the material-to-text check stay in FICCVoice.md V37–V40.

A later check (check mode, or a check after a revision) covers the paragraphs changed and every paragraph whose opening, carry-over or close the change touched; paragraphs untouched since the last check keep that check's result, which the packet attaches. A whole-draft assembly that changes carry-overs, openings or closes re-runs C23 on the affected range only.

- **C23** **Before submitting, check the openings at every layer again on the finished draft.**
  - Without the material or the relation sheet, using only the finished draft and the name table, extract each paragraph's opening, and restate how the piece moves from one judgment to the next.
  - Where the opening is: per C01.
  - Check: cover everything after the opening and read only the opening.
    - It passes when the opening states what its C01 paragraph-type row requires.
  - If you can restate only an attitude, a reading, a count, an evaluation, a subject, or what the block is going to discuss, rewrite it.
  - Where the same conclusion appears more than twice in the openings of adjacent layers, revise per C07.
  - Check these first:
    - An opening that only sets the tone, with no carry-over (C02).
    - A carry-over written as a table of contents, roadmap, or coordinates (C04, C06).
    - Counter-views, the conclusion section, or appendices worked into the carry-over paragraph (C05).
    - A close that repeats the opening or uplifts (C17, C19).
    - Carry-overs and closes forced onto small nodes (C19).
    - A preview in the claim or carry-over paragraph repeated as a later opening, word for word or nearly so: shorten the preview or let the opening add the relation word and the step (C04, C20).
- **C24** **Define once, then combine in short form.**
  - When a paragraph gives the outcomes of two or three indicators, define the indicators by full name once, then write each outcome in one sentence with short names and counts (“三项都不动”, “前两项动、第三项不动”); never restate every full name in every branch, never open with a sentence that announces the outcomes will now be listed, never close with a hedge already given at the definition.
  - More than three outcomes, or outcomes that each need a qualifier, go into a table.
  - Check: count the full indicator names in the paragraph; each appears once.
