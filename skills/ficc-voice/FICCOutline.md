# FICCOutline: Write the outline first, then write from it

Before using the full FICC writing skill set, write the outline first (see O89). “The user” means the author. “You” means the agent applying the rule.

- The outline agent (ficc-outliner) is the only agent that builds the tree. It maintains nodes, numbering, claims, tasks, unfoldings, headings, the name table, order lines, relation lines, and annotation lines.
- The outline agent decides placement, selection, and order per 5 and 6.
- The relation agent (ficc-structurer) judges per FICCStructure.md each relation's type and whether it holds, to the levels above, the same level, the levels below, and distant nodes, and writes the relation sheet.
- FICCCompose.md governs, inside a settled node, how paragraphs open, carry over, unfold, and close.
- FICCVoice.md governs the wording that states settled relations clearly.

Examples and revision examples: references/examples-and-rewrites.md, filed by this file's section numbers with their sources. Rationale: references/FICCOutline-rationale.md.

## 1 The outline is a tree

- **O01** **Build the outline as a tree.**
  - The whole piece is the trunk. Parts, sections, subsections, paragraph groups, paragraphs, and sentence groups, down to complex sentences, are branches.
  - Each branch is a part relative to the level above, and a trunk relative to the level below.
  - Make each block sum up one idea only, and state it in full.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 1.

### 1.1 Nodes and scales

- **O02** **Recognize nodes at eight scales.**
  - The eight scales: whole piece, part, section, subsection, paragraph group, paragraph, sentence group, complex sentence.
  - The clauses inside a complex sentence are leaves.
  - Inside a simple sentence, syntax governs. Do not apply the four positions. Require only a clear sentence core and unambiguous attachment of modifiers, per FICCVoice.md V15, V16.
  - The eight scales are not a hierarchy table.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 1.1.
- **O03** **Tell the trunk from the branch lines.**
  - There is usually only one trunk. To serve it, several branch lines are often added.
  - A branch line, taken as its own trunk, may also have branch lines that serve it.
  - The rules below treat a block the same as a child node and as a parent node.

### 1.2 Four positions

- **O04** **Give every node four positions.**
  - The four positions: claim (required), carry-over (required when the claim is a tone-setting sentence), child nodes (the sub-points), and summary close.
  - The four positions are positions and functions, not four sentences. They are also an upper limit.
  - Small nodes may contract: a short paragraph may omit the carry-over, and the close may fall on the last child node.
  - In a long paragraph, each position may be several sentences.
  - In sections and parts, each position may be one paragraph or several.
  - See FICCCompose.md C01, C17: where the four positions fall in the body text, and which layers must have a close.

### 1.3 Every subtree is complete

- **O05** **Make every subtree complete when read alone.**
  - Every node has its own claim and child nodes, and a close where one is needed.
  - A core subtree read alone is a complete small article.
  - A satellite subtree is structurally complete (it has a claim and relations). It may refer back to the claim it depends on.
  - When several numbered stories form one section, each story is complete, and the collection itself must also have an overall opening and a close.
  - Check: apply the subtree test in O89.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 1.3.

## 2 The root claim: the center of the outline

- **O06** **Start the outline from one central sentence. Set the center by text type.**
  - Argumentative text: the center is a question to answer or a proposition to argue.
  - Narrative text: the center is a conflict, a change, or a thread. For the storytelling branch, see O12.
  - Expository or methodological text: the center is which object the reader is helped to understand, or which task the reader is helped to complete.
  - Mixed text: set the main goal first. Then state how the other modes of writing serve it.
- **O07** **This central sentence is the root claim. Write it as the whole piece's judgment on one question.**
  - The root claim may be as short as one or two sentences.
  - Check: the root claim is one proposition with content, and every other node's task returns to it.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 2.

### 2.1 Define the question, then form the judgment

- **O08** **Define the question first, then form the judgment.**
  - Write the object, the period, the measurement basis, and the question to answer.
  - State which phenomenon is explained, who made what choice under what constraints, and how we judge from that.
  - Check: if the question is not clearly defined, or the text only says it will discuss a topic, there is no main line yet.
- **O09** **When the center is not settled, write the open question or a provisional center first.**
  - For how to write the open question, see O21.
  - For a provisional center, write what it rests on and where the uncertainty lies.
- **O10** **Open the outline with a header, then write the root claim.**
  - Write these items in the header:
    - Whether this is new writing, a check, the addition of new material, or a restructuring.
    - Title and goal.
    - Genre, audience, and approximate length.
    - Content that must be kept, and other constraints.
    - What the reader should understand and believe after reading.
    - For a story, also write what the reader feels. For a method piece, write what the reader can do.
  - Unless commissioned to make trade recommendations, do not write “采取什么行动” (what action to take) into the goal.
  - When not commissioned, do not invent a separate paragraph of action recommendations in the close to fill it out.
  - Do not invent items the user did not give. Leave them blank and mark them on the outline. For the marks, see O38.
- **O11** **Choose the overall structure. State in one or two sentences why you chose it.**
  - Overall structures include build-then-refute, chronological, by participant, and question and answer.
  - Tie the reason for the choice back to the root claim.
  - You may open with a popular claim and turn to the mechanism it fails to explain.
  - Set up a target only when a claim is worth rebutting.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 2.1.
- **O12** **For the storytelling branch, still hang the center on the judgment above it.**
  - This applies to case lecture scripts, first-hand hooks, and the storytelling branch of a diary or series.
  - Write who met what in what situation, and how it later changed.
  - On the outline, this branch's claim line states what it shows. Write the conflict, change, or thread in the unfolding line.
  - See FICCCompose.md C01, C16, C19: how a story opens in the body text, and how it is brought back.

### 2.2 Work the mechanism through

- **O13** **Test whether the claim holds by whether the mechanism works through.**
  - Macro and cross-asset research often follows the money:
    - Who faces what constraint.
    - Where the money comes from, and through what channel.
    - Whose cash flow, balance sheet, or positions it changes.
    - How it affects prices.
  - Sit in the participant's seat and reason forward by its constraints.
  - Where relevant, state the damping and the weakest link.
  - For industry, valuation, institutional, and psychological questions, first explain their own mechanisms.
- **O14** **Choose one route the reader can follow. Do not write the route as the only causal chain.**
  - First follow one channel to the end.
  - Then, where the channels connect, state how another channel joins, offsets, or feeds back.
  - Do not line up parties, financing instruments, price outcomes, and explanatory dimensions as one arrow.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 2.2.
- **O15** **Fill in whichever step of the mechanism is missing.**
  - Keep a step that the material cannot prove as a question or a qualifier.
  - Do not leap over it with one “所以” (so).
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 2.2.

### 2.3 Grade strength by evidence

- **O16** **Let the strength of the root claim and of each sub-point follow the evidence that supports it.**
  - State hard evidence, qualified evidence, and doubtful readings separately.
  - When child nodes differ in strength, state them at the parent node by evidence layer: hard ones first, doubtful ones marked as doubtful.
  - When support weakens, narrow the judgment. Do not pile up similar examples for momentum.
  - Use examples to illustrate a mechanism. They cannot automatically prove that the mechanism is general.
  - See O42: summarizing.
- **O17** **Put a real judgment at the root.**
  - A method or a set of questions may drop to a sub-point under the root.

## 3 Every node states four things clearly

### 3.1 The four things

- **O18** **Make every node state four things clearly.**
  - What it says: this node's claim, one conclusion with content. For how to write it, see O20.
  - What it does for the level above: which part of the parent's question it answers, and why the parent cannot do without it.
  - Why it sits here, and its relation to the same level (O31).
  - What it unfolds by: which child nodes hang below, which existing material it uses, and which material is still missing.
  - Check:
    - If only a title such as “背景”“分析”“风险”“案例”“总结” (background, analysis, risks, cases, summary) is written, the claim has not been written yet.
    - If you cannot say what the node does for the level above, either delete it, or it hangs in the wrong place.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 3.1.
- **O19** **Give a reason for the order, stated through the adjacent nodes' concrete content.**
  - Be able to say why this block is read first and the next block after it.
  - Do not write only a relation name.
  - Where adjacent nodes can be swapped, write that they can be swapped. Do not call every pair of adjacent nodes a progression.
  - See FICCStructure.md 1 to 3: how to judge relation types.
  - When the relation agent returns a placement objection through T0, re-decide here.

### 3.2 The claim has content and covers everything

- **O20** **Write the claim as this block's conclusion, in one proposition with content.**
  - Tone-setting sentences may be used, but not alone as the claim. Follow at once with a carry-over that completes the view.
  - The following are tone-setting, not claims:
    - Only taking a stance (agree or not).
    - Only giving a way to read (how to read it).
    - Only announcing a topic (what to look at next).
    - Only reporting a count (how many points, how many features).
    - Only giving an evaluation (whether it is interesting).
    - Only stating what task this block carries (what this part covers).
  - Check: use the information test in O86.
  - See FICCCompose.md C02: carry-over.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 3.2.
- **O21** **When the evidence cannot yet separate the views, let one question govern.**
  - In the claim or the carry-over right after it, write at which step the disagreement sticks and what observation could separate the views.
  - Do not invent a conclusion for an open question to pass a test or for an elegant main line.
- **O22** **Make the claim cover all child nodes, and state nothing more strongly than the body text.**
  - Do not summarize only the first sub-point.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 3.2.

### 3.3 Record the vertical and the horizontal on the outline

- **O23** **For what the outline records vertically and horizontally, and who records it, see O18, O19, O31, and O63.**

### 3.4 Unfold only as far as needed

- **O24** **Unfold the outline until it is enough to guide the writing, then stop.**
  - At macro planning, you may stop at parts or sections.
  - Before writing a section, unfold it to paragraph groups and paragraphs, and to sentences when needed.
  - Set the number of layers by the content.
  - Do not make all branches equally deep or give them equal numbers of child nodes for formal neatness.
  - Do not split just to fill out the layers. When heading levels run out, still sort out the internal relations.
  - Check: following the outline, you know what each paragraph says, which material it uses, and how it connects to what comes before and after.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 3.4.

## 4 Build the outline top-down

- **O25** **Grow the outline from the root downward. Repeat the same action at every layer.**

### 4.1 Eight steps

- **O26** **Build the outline in eight steps: five to grow it to paragraph groups, then three to set length, write headings, and check the outline.**
  - Step 1: write the outline header and the one-sentence root claim, and choose the overall structure (see 2).
  - Step 2: list the trunk sub-points, and write each one's four things (O18). For the check, see O27.
  - Step 3: decide where satellites and non-trunk nodes hang (see O53). For how counter-views change the judgment, see 6.
  - Step 4: set the root claim's position: it sits at the start of the lead (see O77).
  - Step 5: repeat Steps 2 and 3 for each trunk sub-point, down to paragraph groups or to the layer that is enough (O24).
  - Step 6: allocate length by weight (see O44).
  - Step 7: write headings (see O79, O81).
  - Step 8: write the outline down, check it, then write the body text (see O40).
- **O27** **Check that the trunk sub-points together make up the root claim.**
  - Check:
    - Not too much: no statement that the sub-points cannot support.
    - Not too little: no orphan that fails to feed into the root claim (see O43).
    - No mismatch of strength (see O42).
    - Grouping: for the check, see O45.
- **O28** **When a layer has too many nodes, add another layer.**
  - Keep nodes at one level within ten as far as possible. Above twelve (lists of names excepted), consider splitting and regrouping.
  - See FICCStructure.md 3.2: how to split and regroup.
- **O29** **Tell satellites from the trunk by whether the parent claim can stand alone.**
  - Test and boundary: FICCStructure.md S08 (the cover test, in FICCStructure.md 2.1).
- **O30** **At each layer, first settle this place's trunk and attachment. Then sort out the relations within the level.**
  - How a carry-over paragraph chains the parts: FICCCompose.md C04.

### 4.2 How to write the outline, and where it goes

- **O31** **For each node, write one claim line first, followed by four lines.**
  - Task: what it does for the level above.
  - Order (`顺序：`): the reason for the order. The outline agent settles the final order and writes it per O19.
  - Relation (`关系：`): backfilled from the relation sheet (O18, O63). The outline agent enters it as `关系：和<节点编号>，通过<什么内容>，是<关系库哪一类>`. The sheet gives the relation type and how the relation holds, and only confirms the order reason.
  - Unfolding: child nodes, existing material, and material still missing.
  - For nodes near the leaves, the four lines may merge into one.
  - At the paragraph-group level, merge the task and the order into one sentence on its hanging (挂法). The backfilled relation may join that sentence. Then write which material it uses.
  - Do not write the argument on the outline.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 4.2.
- **O32** **Put a name table at the head of the outline, before any writer starts.**
  - The outline agent writes and maintains it when building or revising the outline.
  - List only words that need a fixed name:
    - Self-coined names (metaphors, labels, short names).
    - Nouns with several names (several market names for the same thing, several measurement bases for one indicator).
  - Do not list standard terms or common expressions.
  - List only names already settled: fixed on the outline, decided by the outline agent per O33–O34, or fixed in the original document; a user-specified name is never changed.
  - Where it is unclear which name to use, the outline agent decides per O33–O34 and records it in the table; only a conflict with a user-specified name goes to T0.
- **O33** **Split the name table into four columns, modeled on the ASD-STE100 dictionary.**
  - 名字 (name): the one name used throughout the piece.
  - 指什么与口径 (referent and measurement basis): indicator name, measurement basis, and who computed the figure; when needed, which node writes it out in full first.
  - 本篇写法 (form used in this piece): one model sentence showing how to define the name at first mention and how to refer back to it afterwards.
  - 不许用的叫法 (forbidden names): synonyms, labels, and abbreviations, including metaphor labels and self-coined abbreviations.
  - A forbidden name may carry a condition in parentheses, such as 题目除外 (except in the title), 单用作指标时 (when used alone as an indicator), or 第一次之后 (after the first mention).
  - The “不许用的叫法” (forbidden names) column feeds the mechanical `--names` check in scripts/voice_check.py.
  - After the table, list how to write institution names: English name or Chinese name.
    - Write foreign personal names, companies, institutions, media, and standard English terms in English. Do not coin Chinese translations.
    - Write Chinese personal names, companies, and institutions in Chinese. For now, write central banks and government agencies in Chinese.
  - See FICCVoice.md 5.2: rules for forbidden names.
- **O34** **Word every node's claim line and 〔标题〕 (heading) line per the name table, always in plain language.**
  - Give each number its indicator name.
  - Give each comparison its object and base period.
  - Do not use coined labels.
  - Do not replace indicators, data series, or groups with coined short labels.
  - The one follow-the-money metaphor of the whole piece (FICCVoice.md 6) may enter claim lines and 〔标题〕 lines in these two cases:
    - First, the point of this node is to introduce the metaphor, and the body text explains it on the spot here.
    - Second, earlier text has already used it widely and explained it.
  - See FICCVoice.md 5.2: how labels are used in the lead and the body text, and how to spell them out on the spot.
- **O35** **When the article cites external material, set up one source-list node at the end.**
  - One writer writes it. It does not count toward length.
  - For each entry, write the institution, the date printed in the original report's body, and the report title.
  - See SKILL.md: checking the body text against the list after assembly.
- **O36** **When outlining an existing draft, summarize it from the original.**
  - First identify how it is actually built. Then state whether this actual structure matches the intended goal.
  - Summarize each claim line from sentences already in the original. Put it in plain language, keep the original strength, and write nothing new.
  - List the name table from the names the original already uses.
  - Where functional headings such as “背景”“分析”“总结” (background, analysis, summary) are used, add one sentence on what the part actually covers.
  - Write such an outline first for style-only edits and conversion to written register as well.
  - Use this outline only to see the original's structure.
- **O37** **When the user gives only body text or material and no outline, build a temporary outline first.**
  - Build it from the existing body text or the writing goal. Mark which nodes are your inference.
  - List gaps that would substantially change the center, the structure, or the attachment of material as open questions for T0.
  - List only the few most critical open questions.
  - For the rest, state your assumptions and carry on.
- **O38** **Write the outline with indentation, numbering, and fixed marks.**
  - Give nodes indentation and numbers, for easy reference.
  - Mark missing content “待补充” (to be supplied). Mark facts that need confirming “待核实” (to be verified).
  - Open the order line with `顺序：` and the relation line with `关系：` (O31).
  - Write each claim line exactly as `- **主旨**：` and put the name table under a `## 名字表` heading, with the O33 columns.
  - Compatibility: in an outline written before 2026-10-17, a relation line that also carries the order reason reads as order and relation written together. Split it into the two lines when you maintain that node.
  - Do not invent story details, motives, quotations, data, or sources.
  - Do not carry a model text's numbers, facts, legal interpretations, and experiences into a new topic.
  - Keep the outline and annotations out of the body text, and do not attach them to the article.
- **O39** **When building or checking an outline, hand back in a fixed order.**
  - Hand back the following, in order:
    - Center and organizing approach.
    - Tree outline.
    - Notes on important relations.
    - Structural problems and suggested revisions.
    - Information or material to be supplied.
  - For a simple task, hand back only the necessary parts.
  - Do not invent relations or problems to fill the format.
  - Point out only problems that actually affect the writing. Give adjustments that can be carried out.
- **O40** **Save the outline, check it, and post it before writing the body text.**
  - T0 sets the save path (SKILL.md) and writes it in the packet.
  - After saving, check the outline per 9.
  - Post it in the conversation first, then write the body text. By default, do not wait for approval.
  - When the user says they want to see the outline first, wait for the user's reply before writing. T0 handles this step.
- **O91** **Claim lines speak the article's language.** 主旨, 任务, 展开 lines and name-table rows are written as the article will say it. Research vocabulary from rulings, ledgers or dissent maps (读法, 判别读数, 对手假设, 反例, 口径 used as a thing) is translated into who thinks what and which number is watched before it enters a line; the name table admits no name for an interpretive move of ours. A ruling that arrives in research vocabulary is carried out in article language and the translation is noted in the placement record. Check: grep the outline's claim lines for `读法|判别读数|对手假设|反例`; zero hits.

### 4.3 Summarizing and length

- **O41** **When any of the seven invariants breaks, go back to that layer and rearrange the child nodes.**
  - This file governs summarizing (O42), completeness (O05), and length (O44).
  - For attachment and hanging, see 5.3. For grouping and re-mention, see FICCStructure.md 3.2 and FICCCompose.md C07.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 4.3.
- **O42** **Make the parent node's claim equal the one thing the child nodes together explain.**
  - Inductive group: the claim is what they have in common.
  - Deductive group and chain: the claim is the conclusion drawn from them.
  - You may add our evaluation. Do not add new facts absent from the child nodes.
  - For strength, see O16. For covering everything and for previews, see O22.
  - Check: apply the parent–child test in O89.
- **O43** **Give every block a destination. Leave no orphans.**
  - Trunk child nodes feed into the parent node's claim.
  - Satellites: place them per the hanging rules in O53.
  - Counter-views, the conclusion section, and appendices hung after the main line feed into the parent node's close, or explain themselves at their own start.
  - Whatever can go nowhere, either delete it or hang it elsewhere (see 5).
- **O44** **Let length follow weight.**
  - A child node's length follows its weight in the parent node's claim, or how much it can change the judgment.
  - Give important branch lines enough mechanism and evidence.
  - Do not let minor background outweigh the trunk just because there is a lot of material.
  - Do not give the sub-point with the weakest evidence the largest node.
  - When a branch line drifts from the original question, first judge whether it deserves its own part, or should be compressed or set aside.
  - Length caps, kept only in the node's 展开 line (the relation sheet and the packet point to it and do not copy it):
    - Give length by writing unit. A writing unit is the block assigned to one writer to finish in one round.
    - In principle, a unit does not exceed the cap of the lowest-level prose section of the body text (2000 Chinese characters).
    - Count only the Chinese characters of the body text. Headings, figure lines, tables, figure notes, and note lines do not count.
    - Write in each node's 展开 (unfolding) line which nodes its unit includes, its cap, and the counting method.
    - When length information is insufficient, state its relative weight against adjacent blocks. Do not make up character counts.
    - Give a cap the writer can count on the spot, such as “不长于相邻那一句” (no longer than the adjacent sentence) or “一句以内” (within one sentence).
    - Give each unit's length cap after designating the full-statement places (O51). For the unit responsible for writing a fact in full, leave room in its cap for writing it in full.
- **O45** **Keep the child nodes of one group at one level of abstraction, on one basis of division, answering the same question.**
  - Grouping does not require equal length.
  - Home rule: FICCStructure.md 3.2.

## 5 Placement: where new stories and changes go on the tree

### 5.1 Ask the task first, then settle attachment

- **O46** **To place a piece of material, first summarize what it actually says. Then ask what task it does here.**
  - This applies to a set of facts, a case, a group of numbers, a figure, or a new story.
  - Do not add details for it.
  - Separate the facts the material states explicitly, the interpretations open to discussion, and the speculation not yet confirmed.
  - Then ask which task it does here:
    - Provide support, or introduce an exception or a counter-example.
    - Explain a mechanism, or make an abstract judgment concrete and tangible.
    - Contrast with or complement existing material.
    - Show a change in a person or a situation, drive action, or escalate conflict.
    - Set up or pay off foreshadowing.
    - Change the reader's understanding, emotions, or expectations.
  - State what more the reader knows after reading: new information, understanding, evidence, or a change in the narrative.
  - Do not place material whose task you cannot state, however relevant it is to the title's topic and however good the story itself is.
  - Do not group paragraphs by source: do not treat “from the same company, the same report, the same month” as a reason to group a paragraph.
  - State the background the reader needs to understand it.
- **O47** **Once the task is set, settle attachment.**
  - Hang it under whichever branch it supports.
  - If it takes several paragraphs to tell and supports one step of the level above, make it its own branch.
  - If it only adds evidence or an example to one step, embed it in that step.
  - If it does the same job as an existing block, handle it per O48.
  - When the position is clear, give it directly.
  - Compare two or three candidate positions only when there is a real disagreement.
  - When comparing, state what task each position can do and at what cost. Then state why you chose this one.

### 5.2 Look at it together with the material on the same branch

- **O48** **Look at it together with the material on the same branch. State what it adds beyond the existing material.**
  - If it only repeats the role of existing material, delete it, merge it, or replace it with the more expressive one.
  - If it offers a new angle or takes a step further, write what it adds and which block it follows.
  - For complement, contrast, or progression, and for writing suggestions, see FICCStructure.md 9.
- **O49** **After placing it, look back at the level above.**
  - Check whether the judgments, transitions, and conclusions above, and the echoes elsewhere, need to change with it.
  - For changing the tree itself, see O62.
  - When material has several uses, see O51 for the main position and the citing places.
  - Do not presume that all new material deserves to go in. For material that does not fit this piece, suggest setting it aside or discarding it, and state why.
  - When the fault lies in the original outline, propose a structural revision. When it is worth it, regroup a part around the revision.
- **O50** **Write the result of handling a new story or new material as a placement record. Hand back its five parts in order.**
  - Write the placement record per references/placement-record-template.md (the single definition of its parts). Then apply part 4 to the outline yourself and list the affected nodes for the relation agent.
  - Affected nodes include this node, the parent node, affected child nodes, siblings whose carry-over or linking-back sentence must change, and distant nodes that may need a back-reference or an echo.
  - For simple material, hand back only the parts that apply (see O39).

### 5.3 Attachment and hanging

- **O51** **Let each piece of material and each sub-point live only in the node it directly supports.**
  - Where it is needed elsewhere, cite it and state what new use it serves there. Do not copy it or retell the whole block.
  - Write the back-reference annotation at the citing place per the relation sheet (see O64).
  - A fact several nodes need: write it in full in the one node it directly supports. Others carry the conclusion and at most one number and refer back.
    - The same fact means a number with its indicator name and base period, a definition, or a judgment.
    - Other nodes means the lead, summaries at each layer, closes, and the table in the changes section.
    - Record in the 展开 (unfolding) lines which node writes it in full. The relation sheet points to that line and does not copy it. It cites the fact itself by materials index number or material position; a disputed or pending fact also carries its question number (I-nn), the ledger id defined in ~/.claude/skills/research-agent/references/analysis.md. The dispatch packet attaches the readable source excerpt.
    - For a fact the outline assigns no full-statement place, each node that uses it writes it in full, not just a back-reference.
- **O52** **Hang content first on the object it directly explains.**
  - Do not let every sentence skip the middle layers to proclaim the whole piece's claim.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 5.3: examples of layer-by-layer service and of a contrast.
- **O53** **Place nodes that are not trunk sub-points by fixed hanging rules.**
  - Evidence, examples, qualifiers: they follow the step they serve.
  - When a contrast neither supports nor rebuts the main line:
    - Hang it after the main line is proven and before the verdict, or put it in an appendix.
    - Do not make it a part at the same level as the trunk sub-points.
  - “反方看法” (counter-views) and reverse branch lines that only limit one step: place them beside that step.
  - Counter-views that can change the judgment of a whole section or of the whole piece:
    - They may come early or late, but concentrate them in one node.
    - Give them enough length before the verdict.
  - Corrections (relative to the previous version): concentrate them in one place, in the conclusion section or the appendix.
  - Background: place it before its first use.
- **O54** **Give evidence against the root claim a home early.**
  - Do not hold material that could overturn the main line until the end as a routine risk note.
  - In a long article, concentrate the weighing into one section when needed.
  - In a short article, it may blend into the relevant paragraphs.
  - For how to weigh counter-views, see O60.
  - For how to write counter-views, see FICCVoice.md 5.1.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 5.3.

### 5.4 Keep separate accounts for each party on the chain, ordered by stage

- **O55** **When telling a financing chain or a transaction, write separate accounts for each party on the chain.**
  - The order in the material is not the order of the article.
  - For each party, state four things:
    - What it owes.
    - What is visible on its statements.
    - What it may lose.
    - When it would lose it.
  - When several institutions describe the same party's accounts, still hang them under that party.
- **O56** **When telling a mechanism that unfolds over time, order it by time stage, divide by role within each stage, and give one sentence per party.**
  - If a party has no fact to write at a stage, leave that party out.
  - See FICCStructure.md 3.3: what each stage gives first, and how stages connect.
  - See FICCVoice.md V15: within one sentence, state the two sides' matters separately.

### 5.5 Facts, interpretation, and conclusions connect

- **O57** **Make facts, interpretation, and conclusions connect.**
  - Write what the material says, how far we infer from it, and on what grounds in between.
  - See FICCStructure.md 2.4: where material of different functions goes, and which measurement bases, qualifiers, and evidence go with each.
- **O58** **Delete material within your permissions. Record each deleted fact item by item in the placement record.**
  - Large sets of reference figures may stay in the existing tables, notes, or source positions.
  - When the user requires complete information, keep it by regrouping and layering. Do not delete unique information for structural neatness.
  - When new writing carries authority to select, delete material that neither advances the trunk nor carries a necessary explanation.
  - Do not add appendices automatically.
  - For facts the placement record keeps, later compress only wording and detail.
  - When a fact really must be deleted, re-examine the placement decision within the selection scope the task allows, update the placement record, and apply the changes.
  - Where this changes relations, list them for T0 to forward to the relation agent, who updates the relation sheet (see FICCStructure.md 9).
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 5.5.

## 6 Maintaining the outline: how counter-views and new material change the tree

- **O59** **This section governs how counter-views and new material change the outline.**
  - For where they go on the outline, see 5.
  - For what relations they have, and to what, see FICCStructure.md.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 6.

### 6.1 Counter-views: give the strongest reason first, then weigh

- **O60** **For a counter-view, first state its strongest reason. Then weigh it.**
  - Judge whether it changes the direction, the timing, the room, the magnitude, or the conditions of application.
  - In new writing or an authorized revision of judgment, when counter-evidence is strong enough to overturn the trunk, change the trunk and the later text that depends on it (see O62).
  - Do not force it into a “长期仍然如此” (still true in the long run) tail.
  - When authorized only for a faithful rewrite, point out this problem separately per the rewrite permissions (see O74).
  - See FICCVoice.md 5.1: how to write counter-views.
  - For how to admit that your own earlier judgment was wrong, see FICCVoice.md V20.
- **O61** **Weigh by criteria.**
  - Explain why differences in time, institutions, parties, or constraints change the outcome.
  - Keep the dominant force, the opposing force, and the conditions that trigger change. Give the reader a grounded judgment.
  - Check: merely attaching one time horizon to each side does not count as handling the contradiction.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 6.1.

### 6.2 When new material changes the judgment, change the tree

- **O62** **When new material changes the judgment, change the tree first, then the text.**
  - When new material reveals a question the original outline missed, add a node and write its four things.
  - When it changes the central judgment of a branch or of the whole piece, change that branch or the root claim first.
  - Then change the dependent nodes and the later text.
  - Do not force new material into the old tree and wrap it up with one “但也要注意” (but note also).
  - When new material offers a better way to organize, reorganize by it.
  - Hand back the revised partial outline.
  - Changing the tree is bound by the rewrite permissions (see O69).
- **O63** **The outline agent applies changes per part 4 of its own placement record. The relation sheet feeds back only relation lines and annotation lines.**
  - Hand back the revised outline and any needed number maps.
  - Do not re-judge relations the relation sheet settles.
  - Where maintenance substantially changes a settled claim, content, or order, list the changes for T0.
  - T0 forwards them to the relation agent to fill in the affected relations. Do not re-check unaffected places.
  - See O50: part 4.

### 6.3 Cross-branch annotations

- **O64** **Add a separate annotation line on the outline for each cross-branch relation, so that distant branches stay connected.**
  - Whether cross-chapter echoes, foreshadowing, contrasts, and dependencies hold, and which kind each is, is judged per FICCStructure.md 5.
  - For those judged to hold, the relation agent writes them into the relation sheet. The outline agent adds annotations under the relevant nodes as given.
  - Format: references/relation-sheet-template.md 3.
  - The foreshadowing annotation names the content the later node pays off; on the page: FICCCompose.md C15.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 6.3.

## 7 Genre scaling and rewrite permissions

- **O65** **When changing genre, change only each layer's length and the number of layers.**
  - The four positions and the seven invariants hold in short commentary, long articles, list months, Q&A, and tutorials.
  - Expanding and compressing add to or subtract from the same tree.
  - Whether you may rebuild depends on the rewrite permissions (see O69).
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 7.

### 7.1 How each genre scales

- **O66** **Scale the outline by genre.**
  - Short commentary: fewer sections are allowed, but the question, the explanation, the judgment, and the necessary qualifiers must all connect.
  - Monthly short piece: a few lines of outline are enough: one root-claim sentence, and two or three sub-points each with task, order, relation, and material.
  - Long article: may unfold several groups of related questions. First set each one's role, then decide how many parts and layers.
  - List month: see O78.
  - Q&A: give the questions premise or progression relations between them. For questions as headings, see O79.
  - Tutorial: organize by the dependencies that learning requires. Make each case explain the knowledge point it serves.
  - Financial case: do not require every case to have the same three segments (participant structure, size estimate, mechanism process).
  - Lecture notes: when keeping established terms and structure, consult references/lecture-terms.md.
  - Diary or series: let the reader see how the same story changes across periods.
- **O67** **Do not take “complete” to mean that background, risks, recommendations, and summary must each get a section.**
  - The four positions do not automatically produce risk sections, recommendations, citation appendices, or new word-count targets.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 7.1.

### 7.2 Expanding and compressing

- **O68** **When expanding, fill gaps first. When compressing, remove repetition first.**
  - When expanding, add the missing links, evidence, and reverse conditions.
  - When compressing, first handle repetition, stray branch lines, and overly dense background.
  - When compressing, keep the trunk's grounds and the qualifiers that would change the judgment.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 7.2.

### 7.3 Rewrite permissions

- **O69** **Let the task's permissions decide what a revision may touch.**
  - In a style-only edit, leave both content and structure untouched.
  - In a style-only edit, merging a note back into the word, number, or sentence it serves counts as wording and is allowed (see FICCVoice.md 5.1).
  - Reorder, split, merge, or adjust layers only under an explicit authorization to reorganize (restructure).
  - When restructuring, check that points, evidence, counter-evidence, qualifiers, figures and tables, and citations each have a destination.
- **O70** **Whenever you rewrite existing text, add no facts the original lacks.**
  - This rule holds whatever the permissions, whether you reorganize or supplement.
  - “Original” means this round's material plus supplementary material already approved for the task. A supplement with a source is not an invented fact.
  - Do not add causation through rhetoric.
  - Keep the attribution and degree words of the original judgment at their original strength.
  - The parties, periods, samples, and inference attributions that come with the original are part of the original sentence. Restoring them is not adding facts.
  - When writing claim, carry-over, and close sentences, only bring forward or gather back conclusions this block already has.
  - Supplied claim paragraphs and the whole piece's lead follow the same rule.
  - Whether to keep the speaker and how to attach the source: per FICCVoice.md 5.1.
- **O71** **Keep author-written sentences verbatim.**
  - The outline agent marks the node on the outline 〔作者亲写·逐字〕 (author-written, verbatim).
  - The writer copies the sentence verbatim and changes no words or punctuation.
  - The relation sheet's “作者原句” (author's original sentence) column cites its node on the outline, not a copy. The dispatch packet attaches the sentence itself.
- **O72** **In new writing or an authorized reorganization, rearrange order, paragraphs, and headings from the content relations.**
- **O73** **In a style-only edit or transcript cleanup, keep the original's content and structure.**
  - Do not change meaning, measurement basis, causation, timing, strength of judgment, or speaker on your own.
  - Handle it per references/written-register.md.
  - Flat layout when the original cannot be arranged into a progressive chain, and how to write a chain: → FICCCompose.md C04.
  - Do not add inferences the original lacks just to form a chain.
- **O74** **When the structure itself has a problem, only point it out and explain it separately. Do not rebuild on your own.**
  - The same holds for problems seen while summarizing the outline from the original.
  - Do not cross the task boundary in the name of improving the structure.
  - Do not delete information originally required to be kept under the pretext of straightening the main line.
  - Authorization to reorganize does not automatically change the protective boundary of facts and judgments.
  - When the original's argument has a gap and you are not authorized to re-research or revise the judgment, point out the gap separately.
- **O75** **Hold the boundary on facts and numbers. Do not correct them under cover of a style edit.**
  - When you find a doubtful fact, verify it or explain it separately per the task permissions.
  - Where the source material has a number, keep the original number. Where a conversion exists, keep its basis.
  - In a style-only edit, do not compute new numbers. Do not use “大概” (roughly) to cover a number without a source.
  - When verification or calculation is separately authorized, handle it per that task.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 7.3.

## 8 Onto the page: the lead and headings

- **O76** **The outline's root becomes the lead. Its second layer becomes headings and section opening sentences.**
  - Lead: see O77.
  - How to write headings: see O79.
  - For the length cap, see O82.

### 8.1 The lead

- **O77** → FICCCompose.md C03 (the lead and its three arrangements)
  - In all three, the heading states a judgment.
- **O78** **In a list month made of several adjacent stories, make the claim in the lead their common question.**
  - The overall title may be a common question, an observation method, or the reason for choosing these stories.
  - Each story then sets up its own main line.
  - Do not force one shared up-or-down conclusion. Do not treat “杂谈” (miscellany) as a license to collect material at random.
  - See FICCCompose.md C05, C18: the list month's carry-over paragraph and the whole piece's close.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 8.1.

### 8.2 Headings

- **O79** **Prefer writing a heading as this block's claim, but do not let it replace the claim paragraph.**
  - Let headings follow the content relations.
  - Paragraph organization: FICCCompose.md C01.
  - Check: reading only the judgment headings, you can read this part's progression.
- **O80** **Let each heading answer one question only, concretely and recognizably.**
  - Do not cram two half-sentences into one heading.
  - A heading may be a noun phrase, a statement, or a posed question.
  - Do not be vague. Do not sacrifice meaning for antithesis.
  - Make sibling headings cover comparable parts of the same higher question.
  - Let the content below deliver on the heading.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 8.2.
- **O81** **The outline agent writes heading text into the node's 〔标题〕 line on the outline.**
  - The writer copies it. The relation agent only reports problems.
  - Check:
    - After writing or changing any 〔标题〕 line, and before handing over the outline, run scripts/voice_check.py with `--names <提纲文件>` (the outline file).
    - How to run it: copy all 〔标题〕 lines by level into a headings-only markdown file, marking the levels with #.
    - An antithetical heading is a hard error. Fix it before handing over.
    - This outline-stage run is without `--clarity`. At the finished-draft stage, `--clarity` only lists headings for review (references/clarity-acceptance.md 3). The two stages differ on purpose.

### 8.3 Sections and ordinals

- **O82** **Start a new section once a knowledge point is finished.**
  - When one knowledge point forms a section, the section's claim states that point's conclusion: what it is, and what it shows.
  - That section is one sub-point.
  - In principle, a lowest-level prose section of the body text does not exceed 2000 characters.
  - Near the cap, divide at a question or argument boundary, by sub-point. Do not cut mechanically, and do not divide by topic.
  - When a large chapter contains several subsections, the chapter's total length is not bound by this cap.
- **O83** **Number headings in the established style, such as “一、（一）1.（1）”. Keep it consistent within a level. Do not skip levels or numbers.**
  - Paragraph organization: FICCCompose.md C10.
  - Do not let long numbering itself become a reading burden.
  - For the check, see O87.
  - See references/format-and-maintenance.md: format details.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 8.3.

## 9 Check the outline

- **O84** **Before writing the body text, check only the outline, in this order.**
  - First see whether the whole piece advances one question (see O85).
  - Then run the information test and extract claims (see O86, O87).
  - Then check the eight most common faults one by one (see O88).
  - Also check that each piece of material's attachment and hanging carry out the placement decision (see 5.3).
  - Check only whether each layer's opening reads as a conclusion with content. Do not check whether the nodes are all present.
  - Openings at each layer of the finished draft: FICCCompose.md C23. Marked relations: the relation agent checks per FICCStructure.md 10.

### 9.1 Center and openings

- **O85** **First see whether the whole piece and each part really advance one question or one clear set of questions.**
  - Check: when a part no longer answers the root question, fix the outline first. Do not check further.
- **O86** **Information test: read only the opening of each layer, and restate the block's conclusion and main reasons.**
  - Check:
    - If you can restate the block's conclusion and main reasons, it passes.
    - Exception: a non-argument node passes when you can restate its conclusion alone (the same exception as FICCCompose.md C01).
    - If you can restate only tone-setting (O20), it fails. Rewrite it.
    - On the outline, this means covering the lower layers and reading only the claim lines.
    - Also check claim lines and 〔标题〕 lines per O34.
    - An opening stated more strongly than the body text, and an opening that covers only the first child node: these two are the most common. Check them first (see O22).
- **O87** **Extract the claims and delete the satellites. What you read should be the whole text abridged at this layer's grain.**
  - In order, read only each node's opening, down to a chosen layer. When the first sentence is tone-setting, include the carry-over sentence.
  - Check:
    - Reading only the headings and the lead gives a one-paragraph abstract.
    - Reading down to section headings plus section opening sentences gives a one-page summary.
    - Reading down to each paragraph's claim gives an abridgement of the whole argument.
    - If what you read is a string of tone-setting (O20), rewrite.
    - If you can restate only that this section has a certain company, figure, or report, and cannot say what they show together, rewrite.
    - Putting the child nodes back should then supply the explanation, the evidence, and the boundaries.
  - Do not delete important counter-evidence and qualifiers because the text reads more smoothly without them.
  - See references/FICCOutline-rationale.md 9.1.

### 9.2 Eight faults

- **O88** **Check the outline one by one for the eight most common faults.**
  - For each one found, point to the exact location and give a fix that can be carried out.
  - For the limits of fault-finding, see O39.
  - The eight faults:
    - Center drift: a part no longer answers the root question.
    - Mixed levels: overall judgments, local causes, and concrete examples mixed at one level.
    - Duplicated function: two nodes do the same job for the level above, and the repeated content plays no new role.
    - Logical leap: a step is missing between two layers, leapt over with one “所以” (so) (see O15).
    - Insufficient support: the judgment is stronger than the material below it, or the inference goes beyond the material.
    - Missing necessary links: a needed explanation, argument, mechanism, counter-view, or qualifier is absent.
    - Awkward order: you cannot say why this block is read first, and needed transitions and cross-branch links are missing.
    - Length imbalance: the shares of length do not match the parts' importance and difficulty of understanding.
  - Check: can the placement reason (what task the node does for its parent) and the order reason of every sibling pair be restated from the neighbours' content?

### 9.3 Write only after the outline passes

- **O89** **Write the outline before writing the body text. For how to write it, see 4.2.**
  - In new writing or an authorized reorganization, the outline must also pass the parent–child and subtree tests below before you write.
  - The grouping test belongs to the relation agent (see FICCStructure.md 10.3).
  - Check:
    - Parent–child test: write the parent node's claim, and list the child nodes' claims beneath it. Check the summary for excess, shortfall, and strength (see O42).
    - Subtree test: take one part and two sections, and read each alone. See whether each is a complete small article (see O05).
